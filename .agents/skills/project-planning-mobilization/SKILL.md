# Project Planning and Mobilization

## Purpose

Turn an authorized initiative into a practical, accountable delivery plan that the Chief of Staff Agent and project stakeholders can use to guide execution.

This skill plans the work. It does not authorize the initiative, approve funding, assign people without authority, or begin external actions.

## Inputs

Use the available information about:

- Approved objective or intended outcome
- Executive sponsor and decision authority
- Business need and expected value
- Scope and exclusions
- Required deliverables
- Stakeholders and affected groups
- Target dates and constraints
- Available budget, people, and technology
- Compliance and governance requirements
- Known risks, assumptions, issues, and dependencies
- Success measures and acceptance criteria

Identify missing information rather than inventing scope, dates, owners, or commitments.

## Planning Approach

Use a delivery approach appropriate to the work:

- Predictive for stable scope and sequenced deliverables
- Iterative for work requiring progressive refinement
- Agile for incremental delivery and frequent feedback
- Hybrid when governance milestones require structure but delivery benefits from iteration

Explain the selected approach when it materially affects the plan.

## Workflow

1. Confirm that the initiative is authorized for planning.
2. Clarify the objective, intended outcome, and decision authority.
3. Define scope, exclusions, constraints, and assumptions.
4. Identify deliverables and measurable acceptance criteria.
5. Break the work into manageable phases or work packages.
6. Identify milestones, dependencies, sequencing, and critical dates.
7. Propose accountable owners without implying unconfirmed assignments.
8. Estimate resource, budget, technology, and procurement needs.
9. Incorporate compliance, security, privacy, accessibility, and governance requirements.
10. Define project governance, decision rights, reporting cadence, and escalation paths.
11. Define success measures, baseline needs, and benefits-tracking responsibilities.
12. Establish initial risks, assumptions, issues, dependencies, and decisions.
13. Assess readiness to begin execution.
14. Return the project plan and decisions needed to the Chief of Staff Agent.

## Required Output

Provide:

1. Executive summary
2. Project objective and expected outcome
3. Authorization and sponsor
4. Scope and exclusions
5. Assumptions and constraints
6. Deliverables and acceptance criteria
7. Delivery approach and rationale
8. Work breakdown or project phases
9. Milestones and target dates
10. Dependencies and sequencing
11. Roles and proposed ownership
12. Resource and budget assumptions
13. Governance and reporting cadence
14. Initial RAID and decision items
15. Success measures and benefits-tracking approach
16. Readiness assessment
17. Decisions needed
18. Immediate next actions

## Ownership

Distinguish among:

- Confirmed owner
- Proposed owner
- Contributing function
- Approver or decision authority
- Consulted stakeholder
- Informed stakeholder

Do not present a proposed owner as committed without confirmation.

## Planning Confidence

Label estimates and dates as:

- Confirmed
- Target
- Preliminary estimate
- Unknown

State the basis and confidence for material estimates.

## Readiness Categories

Recommend one of the following:

- Ready to mobilize
- Ready with conditions
- Additional planning required
- Blocked pending a decision or dependency
- Not authorized to proceed

A readiness recommendation does not authorize execution.

## Handoffs

Recommend that the Chief of Staff Agent involve:

- The Innovation Agent when value, feasibility, or solution design requires further assessment
- The Compliance Agent when requirements, controls, approvals, or formal reviews must be incorporated
- The Data Analyst Agent when baselines, forecasts, measures, or benefits analysis are needed

## Guardrails

- Do not invent authorization, scope, budget, dates, resources, or owner commitments.
- Do not start work, contact stakeholders, assign resources, or make purchases without authorization.
- Do not treat a target date as a committed deadline.
- Do not conceal missing dependencies or unrealistic constraints.
- Do not mark planning assumptions as approved decisions.
- Distinguish planning readiness from approval to execute.
- Escalate unresolved scope, funding, ownership, compliance, and decision-authority gaps.
