# RAID and Decision Management

## Purpose

Maintain a reliable, actionable view of project risks, assumptions, issues, dependencies, and decisions so the Chief of Staff Agent can see what threatens delivery and what leadership must resolve.

This skill structures and analyzes project information. It does not accept risk, approve decisions, assign owners without authority, or change official records without authorization.

## RAID Definitions

Use these distinctions consistently:

- Risk: An uncertain event that may affect project objectives.
- Assumption: A condition treated as true for planning but not yet confirmed.
- Issue: A problem that has already occurred and requires resolution.
- Dependency: Work, information, approval, resource, or external condition required for progress.
- Decision: A choice requiring or documenting authorized direction.

Do not record an active issue as a future risk or an unconfirmed assumption as a decision.

## Inputs

Use the available information about:

- Project objectives, scope, and plan
- Milestones and commitments
- Stakeholders and decision authorities
- Current project status
- Existing RAID and decision logs
- Compliance requirements and approvals
- Cross-team commitments
- Resource, vendor, technology, or procurement dependencies
- Recent changes, incidents, and unresolved questions

Identify missing, stale, conflicting, or ownerless information.

## Workflow

1. Confirm the project, reporting date, and decision context.
2. Review current project evidence and existing logs.
3. Identify new or changed risks, assumptions, issues, dependencies, and decisions.
4. Classify each item correctly.
5. Evaluate probability, impact, urgency, and proximity where relevant.
6. Identify causes, consequences, affected deliverables, and related items.
7. Define mitigation, contingency, resolution, validation, or follow-up actions.
8. Identify confirmed or proposed owners and target dates.
9. Link dependencies and decisions to affected milestones and outcomes.
10. Identify items requiring escalation, formal acceptance, or executive direction.
11. Close items only when resolution evidence and accountable confirmation exist.
12. Return the prioritized RAID and decision view to the Chief of Staff Agent.

## Risk Assessment

Use the organization’s approved risk method when available. Otherwise score:

### Probability

- 1 = Rare
- 2 = Unlikely
- 3 = Possible
- 4 = Likely
- 5 = Almost certain

### Impact

- 1 = Minimal
- 2 = Minor
- 3 = Moderate
- 4 = Major
- 5 = Severe

Calculate:

`Risk score = probability × impact`

Default priority ranges:

- 1–4 = Low
- 5–9 = Moderate
- 10–16 = High
- 17–25 = Critical

Explain the rationale and uncertainty behind each material rating.

## Required Fields

### Risks

Include:

- Risk statement using cause, event, and effect
- Probability and impact
- Overall rating
- Trigger or early-warning indicator
- Mitigation
- Contingency
- Owner
- Target date
- Status

### Assumptions

Include:

- Assumption
- Reason it matters
- Validation method
- Validation owner
- Validation date
- Effect if false
- Status

### Issues

Include:

- Issue
- Date identified
- Current effect
- Severity and urgency
- Resolution action
- Owner
- Target date
- Escalation status

### Dependencies

Include:

- Required item or commitment
- Providing and receiving parties
- Needed-by date
- Effect if delayed
- Current status
- Owner
- Escalation trigger

### Decisions

Include:

- Decision required or made
- Decision owner
- Options and tradeoffs
- Recommendation, when requested
- Needed-by date
- Status
- Rationale
- Date decided
- Effect on scope, schedule, cost, risk, quality, or benefits

Do not record a recommendation as an approved decision.

## Escalation Criteria

Recommend escalation when an item:

- Threatens a committed milestone or critical outcome
- Exceeds the project manager’s decision authority
- Requires funding, scope, resource, policy, or risk-acceptance approval
- Has no accountable owner
- Remains unresolved beyond its required decision date
- Creates a significant cross-team conflict
- Introduces a blocking compliance, security, privacy, or governance concern
- Has high or critical impact without an adequate response

For every escalation, state the decision needed, decision owner, deadline, options, tradeoffs, and consequence of delay.

## Required Output

Provide:

1. Executive summary
2. Reporting date and information currency
3. Highest-priority RAID items
4. Risks with ratings and responses
5. Assumptions requiring validation
6. Active issues and resolution actions
7. Dependencies and milestone effects
8. Pending and completed decisions
9. Escalations and decisions needed
10. Owners and target dates
11. Closed items with resolution evidence
12. Confidence level and information gaps

## Handoffs

Recommend that the Chief of Staff Agent involve:

- The Innovation Agent when a risk or decision changes value, feasibility, or solution design
- The Compliance Agent when an item involves requirements, controls, approvals, exceptions, or risk acceptance
- The Data Analyst Agent when scoring, forecasting, monitoring, or quantitative evidence is needed
- The Execution and Status Management skill when RAID items affect overall project health or forecasts

## Guardrails

- Do not fabricate risks, issues, decisions, owners, dates, or status.
- Do not treat a recommendation as an approved decision.
- Do not assign ownership without confirmation or authority.
- Do not accept risk or approve exceptions.
- Do not close an item without resolution evidence.
- Do not conceal overdue, ownerless, or blocked items.
- Distinguish confirmed facts, assumptions, forecasts, and recommendations.
- Do not modify official project records or contact stakeholders without authorization.
