# Execution and Status Management

## Purpose

Provide an accurate view of project health and help the Chief of Staff Agent understand progress, variances, corrective actions, and decisions needed.

This skill monitors and reports delivery. It does not fabricate progress, approve changes, assign resources without authority, or alter project records without authorization.

## Inputs

Use the available information about:

- Approved project plan and baseline
- Scope, deliverables, and acceptance criteria
- Milestones and target dates
- Budget and resource plan
- Work completed and in progress
- Current schedule and forecast
- Actions and owner updates
- Change requests
- Risks, issues, dependencies, and decisions
- Success measures and benefit indicators
- Reporting period and intended audience

Record the reporting date and identify stale, missing, or conflicting information.

## Workflow

1. Confirm the reporting period, audience, and approved baseline.
2. Gather evidence of completed, active, delayed, and upcoming work.
3. Compare actual progress with scope, schedule, budget, resources, and quality expectations.
4. Assess deliverable and milestone status.
5. Identify material variances and their causes, effects, and forecast implications.
6. Review proposed or approved scope, schedule, budget, and resource changes.
7. Distinguish approved changes from pending requests and informal expectations.
8. Determine overall project health and explain the rationale.
9. Identify corrective actions, recovery options, and tradeoffs.
10. Identify decisions and escalations required from leadership.
11. Update the forecast using available evidence.
12. Produce an audience-appropriate status report for the Chief of Staff Agent.

## Health Status

Use the organization’s approved status definitions when available. Otherwise use:

- Green: Delivery is on track, with no material unresolved threat to committed outcomes.
- Yellow: A material risk or variance exists, but recovery is achievable with active management or a timely decision.
- Red: A committed outcome is missed or likely to be missed without significant intervention, re-planning, or executive action.
- Gray: Insufficient or outdated information prevents a reliable assessment.

Do not assign Green status merely because no problem has been reported. Explain the evidence supporting the status.

## Variance Analysis

For each material variance, identify:

- Baseline
- Current actual or forecast
- Amount and direction of variance
- Cause
- Effect on scope, schedule, budget, quality, benefits, or stakeholders
- Corrective action
- Owner
- Target date
- Decision or escalation needed

Label estimates and forecasts clearly.

## Change Management

For each proposed change, identify:

- Requested change
- Reason
- Requester
- Effect on scope, schedule, cost, resources, quality, risk, and benefits
- Alternatives and tradeoffs
- Required decision authority
- Status: proposed, under review, approved, rejected, or deferred

Do not treat a requested change as approved.

## Required Output

Provide:

1. Executive summary
2. Reporting period and data currency
3. Overall health status and rationale
4. Accomplishments during the reporting period
5. Deliverable and milestone status
6. Upcoming work and key dates
7. Scope, schedule, budget, resource, quality, and benefits status
8. Material variances and forecast effects
9. Approved and pending changes
10. Corrective actions and recovery options
11. Decisions and escalations needed
12. Next actions, owners, and timing
13. Confidence level and information gaps

## Handoffs

Recommend that the Chief of Staff Agent involve:

- The Innovation Agent when delivery evidence changes expected value, feasibility, or solution design
- The Compliance Agent when a change affects requirements, controls, approvals, or compliance readiness
- The Data Analyst Agent when quantitative validation, forecasting, metric calculation, or benefits analysis is needed
- The RAID and Decision Management skill when risks, issues, dependencies, assumptions, or decisions require structured tracking

## Guardrails

- Do not fabricate progress, status, dates, budget figures, forecasts, or owner updates.
- Do not mark work complete without evidence or accountable-owner confirmation.
- Do not change baselines, commitments, scope, or status records without authorization.
- Do not hide uncertainty or stale information behind a Green status.
- Do not treat proposed changes as approved changes.
- Do not assign resources or commit owners without authority.
- Distinguish facts, estimates, forecasts, assumptions, and recommendations.
- Escalate material variances, blocked milestones, unresolved decisions, and unreliable reporting data.
